# The AAS volumes sample, 1909–2012: the phase-2b-i report

Generated by `npx tsx tools/acta-volumes-report.ts sample` from `data/documents/*.json` and `tools/fixtures/acta/` on 2026-09-13 —
the report of phase 2b-i of [#25](https://github.com/CatholicOS/cmddr/issues/25) as the
[acta volumes spec](../specs/2026-09-13-acta-volumes-design.md) §6 defines it. Six sources were chosen for their variance
(spec §5): the chronological-index pages of AAS 1 (1909), 9-I (1917), 23 (1931), 50 (1958) and 70 (1978), extracted from
the whole-volume OCR PDFs by `tools/fetch-acta.sh` in pypdf's layout mode, and the 2012 *Index generalis* PDF, extracted
whole as in phase 1 (`tools/fixtures/acta/README.md` records page ranges, modes and retrieval). Each is parsed
(`tools/src/acta/index.ts`, generalised across the century's typography and OCR), classified (`categories.ts`), its pope
headings mapped (`popes.ts`), and matched to the shelf records of Pius X → Benedict XVI by issuer, date and incipit
(`match.ts`); what did not match, in a category the registry creates from the *Acta* for that pope, became a document
(`create.ts`, the phase-2a rule and guard unchanged), and everything else is listed here with its reason. The join and the
creator run over every source at once, as the harvest does; the ten index PDFs of 2015–2024 keep their own report
(`2026-09-12-acta-join-2015-2024.md`, regenerated). The *belief* beside an unmatched entry is the script's heuristic; the
reading in the prose is the author's.

### The reading

1. **The OCR of 1909 and 1917 lost the page column, and the PDFs carry no image to check it against.** The vatican.va
   `-ocr.pdf` volumes are typeset from the OCR text (Times fonts, no image object on any page checked), so their
   rendering reproduces the text layer's losses: on most index pages of AAS 1 and AAS 9-I the page column is absent
   (p. 839 of vol. 1 renders two page numbers at its foot and none above them). The parser opened 113 entries in 1909
   and 98 in 1917-I and could close 2 and 37 with a page: the rates of 7.4 % and 90.2 % measure the
   fixtures the files can yield, and the 172 entries opened without a page are listed in §3 as defects, each
   with its date and text. Nothing is cited without a page: an `acta` reference needs one. The page numbers of these
   acts are in the volumes' *Index generalis rerum* (AAS 1 p. 833: *Litterae Apostolicae, 197, 229, 245 …*), listed per
   category in page order without dates -- a join by position that this phase does not attempt. The same OCR reads
   `1910` for 1916 on nine letters of Benedict XV in 1917-I, which the creator holds as dated before his election (§9).
2. **1909 prints no bare incipits and nests a table of contents.** The first volume's index names an act by its description
   (*Dioecesis Rockfordiensis in Statibus foederatis Americae septentrionalis conditur*), and prints an incipit only in
   guillemets after a genre word (*Constitutio « Sapienti Consilio »*, *Litt. encycl. « Communium rerum »*), so the fixture is
   parsed with `bareIncipits: false` and a description is never read as an incipit; the table of contents of *Sapienti*
   *Consilio* (the offices of the Curia, the *Lex propria* and the *Ordo servandus*, 77 lines) is consumed as sub-items of
   one entry. Two of those sub-items are acts of their own -- the *Lex propria S. R. Rotae et Signaturae Apostolicae* (29 June
   1908, AAS 1 p. 20) and the *Ordo servandus in SS. Congregationibus* (29 September 1908, p. 36) -- which the index lists
   only as chapters of the constitution; they are a finding, not entries.
3. **The columnar layout dates some acts to the month.** From 1909 to 1931 the index prints ANNO, MENSE and DIE in three
   columns and leaves a column blank for a ditto; a printed month with a blank day (*1917 Iul. Universalis Ecclesiae*
   *procuratio*, AAS 9-I p. 595, blank in the rendering too) is an act the index dates to the month only. 20 such
   entries in the sample (§1) are kept with a month-precision date, matched by incipit within the month where the shelf
   supplies the day (§5, *incipit-month*), and never created (§7, *unresolvable date*). An OCR-misread day (*» Mai. 80*,
   1978) reads the same way and is reported (§3).
4. **pypdf's default mode breaks the columns; its layout mode keeps them, and interleaves one page.** In the default mode the
   text layer of the OCR'd volumes emits each date column as a run of its own -- every month of the page, then every day,
   then the entries -- and no entry can be dated; the layout mode keeps each date on the line of its entry, at the cost of
   positional padding the parser ignores. On AAS 23 p. 531, where the OCR's line boxes overlap (the spec's "run-together"
   page), the layout mode interleaves two entries' words on one line and lands page numbers on the wrong entries, so
   `fetch-acta.sh` falls back to the default mode for that page (README) and the parser splits the run-together line at
   each page number a date follows: 1931 parses at 100.0 %, and Pius XI's four encyclicals of 1931 cite their pages.
5. **1958 and 1978 are clean and print toponym and incipit both.** 91.3 % and 98.8 % overall, 98.5 % and
   100.0 % over the harvested categories: what 1958 loses below 95 % are the numbered items of its two consistories
   (*I. Consistorium secretum 393*, *II. Optio Ecclesiarum 393* …) and the *Possessio Romanae Cathedralis* line of John XXIII's
   part, which end in page numbers and are not acts. The constitutions read *SANTAREMENSIS (Obidensis). Cum sit. -* (1958) and
   *BOACENSIS. - Cum tempora.* (1978), so a document created from them mints from the incipit (§8). The 2012 index parses as
   2015–2016 do (92.6 % overall, 100.0 % over the harvested categories; the lines below the rate are the
   journeys section's undated *Die N.* lines, as in phase 1).
6. **AAS 9 part II carries no chronological index.** The second part of 1917 is the *Codex Iuris Canonici* itself, with the
   constitution *Providentissima Mater Ecclesia* (27 May 1917, p. 5) before it and the Code's own index after; no fixture
   exists for it, and `acta.part` is therefore `"I"` on every 1917 reference. The one act is on the bulls shelf
   (`mag:benedict-xv/providentissima-mater-1917`) and could take a hand-curated reference (*AAS 9-II (1917) 5*) in 2b-ii.

### The reading

1. **131 references written, every one from a quoted index line (§12):** 1 curated, 19 incipit, 1 incipit-month, 22 toponym, 88 unique. The
   *incipit-month* matches are the month-only entries of §1.3; the *toponym* and *incipit* ones include the claims the
   evidence rule resolved (`match.ts`): where several constitutions of one day are entered against one shelf record of the
   day (10 November 1977: *Avkaënsis*, *Mohaleshoekensis*, *Ambikapurensis* against the shelf's *Avkaensis*), the entry the
   record names keeps the match and the others are released to the creator instead of all three being withheld.
2. **147 documents created** (§8) -- `rp:benedict-xv` 8, `rp:benedict-xvi` 13, `rp:paul-vi` 28, `rp:pius-x` 2, `rp:pius-xi` 60, `rp:pius-xii` 36 -- and 97 entries held (§9), 7 of them by the
   duplicate guard and 2 by the OCR rule. 61 constitutions print toponym and incipit both (§1.5), so almost no provisional id is minted from the
   volumes; the *Epistulae* are created only where the pope's letters shelf is harvested (Pius XI, Pius XII, John Paul I in this
   sample) and held elsewhere (§9, *shelf not harvested*).
3. **A volume can reprint an act another volume already published, and a page can open two acts.** The 2020 index lists
   Benedict XVI's *Ibi vacabimus* (3 July 2011) at AAS 112 (2020) 479, and the 2012 index the same letter at AAS 104 (2012)
   482: two references to one act. On this PR's first run neither was created -- the id-collision rule held both -- and
   which is the citation of record was left to phase 2b-ii-c, which decided it (`ACTA_REPRINTS`, curation.ts: the first
   printing, unless the volume marks the later as a correction; the 2020 fascicle re-prints the letter without a note), so
   the 2012 entry is created and the 2020 one is a reprint (§5). And AAS 70 (1978) p. 150 opens two short apostolic letters (*Sacra illa*, 9 January; *Quoniam beatissima*,
   11 January -- the volume's p. 150 prints both under one running header), which invariant 25's premise did not foresee: the
   page is curated in `ACTA_SHARED_PAGES` (`curation.ts`) with the page quoted, and the invariant exempts exactly those two.
   A page number the OCR misread with a leading zero (*030* for 930, AAS 50 p. 1035) is reported rather than cited.
4. **Documents of the sample's popes dated in the volume years without a reference** (§11): mostly the shelves' own December
   acts (the next volume's), the acts the cropped pages of 1909 and 1917 cannot cite, and the classes the index files under
   a category the registry does not attempt.

Decisions the spec left open, taken here with the evidence beside each row of `categories.ts`: *Epistula encyclica* (1958,
*Ad Apostolorum Principis*) maps to the encyclicals row, since vatican.va's encyclicals shelf carries the act; the two-line
*Adhortatio ad populorum belligerantium moderatores* (1917, *Dès le début*) to the exhortations row, since the
apost_exhortations shelf carries it, unlike 2019's bare *Adhortatio*; *Motu proprio* alone (1909–1958) to the motu proprio
row; *Apostolicae sub plumbo litterae* (1917) to the *sub plumbo* row; *Epistolae* to *Epistulae*, whose harvestedness becomes
`partly` (the letters shelf is harvested for five popes), so the matcher attempts every *Epistula* and the creator creates
only where the pope's letters shelf is harvested; *Nuntii scripto dati* (1958, 1978) to *Nuntii*, since Paul VI's are the
harvested annual series; *Nuntii radiophonici* / *Nuncium radiophonicum* / *Nuntii radiotelevisifici* to a new row mapping to
`message` and `urbi-et-orbi` as `partly` -- the Christmas and Easter *Urbi et Orbi* among them are on the harvested urbi
shelves and match (§12) -- created from never, and counted for #27 (`medium`) rather than applied; *Sermo*/*Sermones* to a
new row of the speeches class, not harvested; *Nuntii gratulatorii* and *Nuntii telegraphici* to rows of their own, not
harvested; *Acta Sacri Consistorii* and *Sacra Consistoria* to *Consistoria* (the parser no longer takes the former for a
part heading); *Chirographe* (OCR) to *Chirographa*; *Homilia*, *Conventio*, *Nuntius televisificus*, the plural
*Adhortationes apostolicae postsynodales* and the 2012 *Litterae Apostolicae «Motu proprio» datae* to their rows. (Since
phase 2b-ii-a the bare *Adhortatio* row maps to the exhortation class as `partly` -- AAS 46 (1954) prints *I rapidi progressi*
under it -- so the 2019 joint appeal on Jerusalem is held by the creator as a category not created from the *Acta* rather
than counted as non-harvested.)
