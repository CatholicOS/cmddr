### The reading

1. **Where the join fires it is evidenced.** 222 matches: 208 the only candidate of the class on the
   date, 14 told apart by the incipit slug, 0 by the toponym (no two constitutions of one date needed it). Every
   one rests on an index line quoted in §10,
   and every Francis encyclical and exhortation of the ten volumes is among them. Nothing was written that the rules could not
   evidence: the 40 ambiguous entries (§4) and the 12 documents two entries claim (§5) stay without a reference.
2. **The Francis shelves are selections, and the index is the record.** The index names 117 apostolic constitutions and 206
   apostolic letters over 2015–2024; vatican.va's apost_constitutions and apost_letters shelves carry 49 and 59 (plain) for the
   same pontificate, and the *bulls* shelf none of the 63 canonisation decretals. Almost every unmatched entry of §6 is a
   **shelf gap** of this kind, and every one prints an incipit, a date and a page: the material for the phase-2 *AAS-only*
   documents is in these tables already.
3. **The index's chronology is not reliable at the ditto mark.** Three acts the shelf does hold are entered under the wrong
   month (*Vultum Dei quaerere* reads 29 July for 29 June, *Episcopalis communio* 15 May for 15 September, *De concordia inter
   Codices* 31 March for 31 May; §6, curated readings), so matching by date alone cannot find them. Phase 2 should look up an
   entry's incipit against the issuer's documents when the date finds nothing — to *report* a candidate, as the ±1-day rule does,
   not to match it — and a curated table can then carry the three.
4. **The shelf truncates incipits; the index does not.** *Ad aptius consulendum* (2015-03-19) is *Ad aptius* on the shelf, and
   *Ad aptius* (2015-12-18) is *Ad aptius consulendum* — the truncation runs both ways — and *Nos, qui successimus* is *Nos, qui*.
   All three are ambiguous only for this reason (§4). The 25 Tarragona beatification letters of 13 October 2013, provisional on
   the shelf because vatican.va prints no incipit for them, each have one in the index (*Spiritus Domini*, *Qui enim voluerit*,
   …); telling the seven shelf records apart needs the documents' own text, which is a RECOVERED_INCIPITS job for the owner.
5. **Messages need an occasion, not a date.** The index carries 391 *Nuntii* (video messages included) and 113
   matched, among them the twenty Christmas and Easter *Urbi et Orbi* the index files as *Nuntius et Benedictio* (the one
   category that maps to two registry classes, `message` and `urbi-et-orbi`). Where two *Nuntii* fall on the date of one series
   message the match is withheld (§5), and two series messages on one date are ambiguous (§4, 8 December 2016): the entry's
   description names the *Dies Mundialis* every time, so a series discriminator is the phase-2 rule to add — or the
   pont-messages harvest (#4) first, which gives every occasional *Nuntius* a candidate.
6. **Filing differences, not defects.** Three acts the index files as *Motu proprio datae* are on apost_letters only, so the
   registry carries no `motu-proprio` characteristic and the class rule declines them (§6); the four *sub plumbo* letters of
   28 November 2020 (cardinalatial titles) are on no harvested shelf, and the shelf files that kind as apostolic letters when
   it files them at all. Whether the characteristic should follow the index is the owner's call; the matcher was not
   loosened to decide it.
7. **Source defects the parser sees** (§3): a constitution whose day is not printed (*Chengden.*, 2018) and one whose page is
   not (*Ioinvillen.*, 2024) — both in a harvested category, both unrecoverable from the text layer — and OCR slips elsewhere
   (a page split `76 4`, ditto marks read `? ?`, a page glued to a footnote digit). None is absorbed.
